LuminaBox tracking

A small sensor unit that tells you where a container is and what it has been through, over whatever link is available.

Know where the box is and how it has been treated

LuminaBox is a small sensor unit that attaches to a container, a tanker or a vehicle. It records where it is and what it has been through, including shocks, vibration, temperature and humidity, and sends that to a client-owned and controlled server, for maximum data control and security. You get a live picture of everything moving around a site.

Put that next to traffic and infrastructure models and you can test ideas before you pour concrete. Would a dedicated truck lane really get fuel to the pads faster? Would more rail?

A small box that tells you how its cargo is doing

LuminaBox clips onto payloads, vehicle sections, containers and tankers, whether they are sitting in storage or on the move. It feels for knocks, vibration, temperature and humidity, tracks its position, and reports to a client-owned and controlled server backend. LuminaTwin uses that feed to see the whole site at once.

Let the trucks tell you which roads are broken

When a fuel tanker with a LuminaBox hits a pothole, the sensor feels the jolt and reports exactly where it happened. Do that across a whole convoy and a repair list writes itself.

Tracking ships when GPS can't be trusted

Around a fifth of the world's oil passes through the Strait of Hormuz, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. At its narrowest the strait is about 21 nautical miles wide, and the shipping lanes are only two miles wide in each direction. That is a natural bottleneck, and jamming and spoofing of satellite navigation have been widely reported in the region.

A tracker that trusts GPS alone can be led astray. LuminaBox only takes a GPS fix about every ten minutes, which saves power and extends battery life, and an inertial sensor fills in the motion between fixes. That also gives it a cross-check: when a new fix disagrees with where the inertial sensor says the box should be, the box can flag the fix as suspect.

The inertial sensor can cover a short outage, but it drifts, so it is no substitute for GPS over a long one. The longer GNSS is untrusted, the more uncertain the position becomes, and the model shows that uncertainty growing instead of hiding it.

The lanes, spacing rules and interference zones are notional, not real incident data, and the drift model is simplified.

What the data can tell you

The client's server receives readings from the accelerometer, the GPS and the environmental sensor, and it can pass them to LuminaTwin. Pick a sensor to see what can be worked out from it.

Into LuminaTwin

Real shocks and travel times replace assumptions in the traffic and road models. Real temperature history feeds the cargo-condition view. The result is a twin that is calibrated by what the fleet actually experiences.
